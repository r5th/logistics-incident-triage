Incident triage: design directions, round 1

Three directions for the Queue and Incident detail, each at 1440 and 390 wide, light and dark. Report texts are synthetic paraphrases of build-split Scenarios; every probability and metric is a placeholder. The roll assigned Tachograph; Route sign was my own top candidate; Interchange is the challenger that held one axis. Results is sketched in the recommended direction only.

Queue

Show the reviewer where attention goes first: what waits in Review, how urgent it is, and where everything else was routed.

TachographTime on the desk is the signal: every report carries a tachograph-style trace, so a long wait in Review shows before you read a word.
Route signRecommendedRead it like a road sign: Destination is a direction plate, Review is a yellow diversion, Urgent is a warning triangle.
InterchangeEvery Destination is a line and Review is the interchange: the Queue is grouped by line, and each report's route is drawn from received to Destination.

Incident detail

Let a reviewer check the proposed decisions against the Evidence and the Review Triggers, then accept, override or escalate.

TachographThe report's time on the desk as a trace above the decision history; decisions read as a ruled ledger of value, runner-up and threshold.
Route signRecommendedThe header is the sign: Urgent, Review, then the Destination plate; actions sit beside it, Evidence and probabilities below.
InterchangeA route diagram heads the page (Received, Triage, Review, Destination), then report, decisions and triggers in three columns.

Results (sketch)

Let the assessor trust the numbers: three approaches side by side against the targets, the operating-point curve with v1 and v2, and every Missed Urgent case.

Route signRecommendedLower fidelity, recommended direction only; targets show met or missed with an icon and a word, never colour alone.

Why Route sign

Pick: Route sign. It answers the demo's question ("where does this report go, and why?") in the vocabulary every UK road user already reads. A Destination plate, a yellow diversion for Review and a warning triangle for Urgent read at thumbnail size and in a screen-share, and the layout stays a standard rail-and-table tool the assessor needs no lesson for.

What it borrows:

  • From Tachograph: a "waiting" time in the Queue (minutes since received for anything awaiting review) and the ruled, dense table discipline, with fewer type sizes doing more work.
  • From Interchange: the stacked probability bar that shows the runner-up beside the top choice, and the "/" shortcut to jump to a report id.

Against the alternatives:

  • Tachograph: the trace ranks by age, but triage ranks by urgency. A fresh urgent report shows as a hairline tick and looks less important than an old routine one, which is the wrong first impression for this product.
  • Interchange: six line colours on top of the status colours overload colour, and grouping by Destination scatters the reports waiting in Review across six boxes, so "what needs me now" stops being one list.

Honest risk of the pick: it is the most familiar of the three. The plates are its only signature, so the build has to keep them crisp and use them only for Destination and Routing Mode, or the screen turns into a themed motorway.
